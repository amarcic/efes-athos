Apparatus criticus

l. 1: According to Dionysopoulos, the second and third numerals of the date are later additions, probably painted after the fire of 1871 (John Komnenos in his proskynetarion transmits only the first numeral - ζ=7000 - which means that the other two were already illegible at the end of the 17th century). Dionysopoulos has argued that the date 7048=1539/40 is incompatible with the biographical data of Alexander II of Kakheti and has suggested a date between 1561 and 1574. If this is correct, the date would have been any number between 7069 and 7083. Thus, the second digit may have been either ξ, ο, or π; the final digit could have been any number between α (=1) and θ (=9), including ς = 6. It is conceivable that it was indeed an η as it is in the preserved text edited above.

Translation

In 7048 (1540) the present holy building of the venerable and royal Monastery of Philotheou (dedicated to) the all-holy Mother of God called Evangelistria (the Virgin of the Annunciation) was renewed by the most revered King Leon of Kakheti of Iberia and his son Alexander for the salvation of their souls (Allison).
